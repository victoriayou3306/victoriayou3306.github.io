The 2026 ASPE Student Challenge was to design a nanoindenter using modular Mech Blocks and a leaf-spring mechanism to determine material properties like hardness and elastic modulus. We were given a Berkovich Indentor, Capacitance Probe, Voice Coil Actuator, and Micrometer stage. We also had Cu substrates to test our indenter before the competition to determine a mystery sample's material. A basic LabView control dashboard was provided for actuation and measurement, and we had to build the indentation control loop around it. The Oliver-Pharr method was used for data analysis.

Justin and I originally met at our summer internship at KLA, and someone on the Mechatronics Team introduced us to ASPE. This conference was incredibly informative and fun, even though pretty much everything went wrong...(see last section), and we were able to meet so many incredible people. The community was unbelievably patient with us, and we are extremely grateful to Vy Tran, the KLA team, and the whole ASPE board and society.

Mechanical Design

Learning about flexure designs was really interesting, and to be honest, it took a while for us to even understand the project principles. We started a few CAD designs, thinking about minimizing the moving mass to reduce deflection due to gravity, as well as lowering the resonance frequency. We were given 10 leaf springs to use, but wanted to minimize their usage because a. we would probably bend and damage a few, and b. the notion that the indentation force being a larger percentage of our actuation force (compared to force in the flexure mechanism) would improve the precision of our measurements.
